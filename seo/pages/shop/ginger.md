## Fresh or dried?

Ginger is traded in two broad forms, and they serve different buyers:

- **Fresh ginger** — whole rhizomes for retail, food service and juicing. Buyers look for firm, plump "hands" with smooth skin and no sprouting.
- **Dried ginger** — sliced or whole ginger dried for spice grinding, extraction and long shelf life.

## What makes good export ginger

<!-- image: ginger-cross-section.jpg alt="Fresh ginger cut open to show pale yellow, low-fibre flesh" -->

When you assess a ginger offer, these are the points that matter:

- **Hand size and weight.** Larger, well-formed hands are preferred for retail display.
- **Skin finish.** Clean, unbroken skin with minimal soil. Some buyers want ginger washed; others prefer it unwashed for longer keeping.
- **Fibre and flesh.** Mature ginger is more fibrous and pungent; younger ginger is juicier and milder. Processors and juicers often have a clear preference.
- **Moisture and firmness.** No shrivelling, soft spots or mould.
- **Pungency and aroma.** Important for spice and beverage applications.
