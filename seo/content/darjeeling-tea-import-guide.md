# How to Import Darjeeling Tea from India: A Buyer's Guide

Darjeeling tea is one of the few teas in the world protected by a Geographical Indication (GI). Only tea grown and processed in designated gardens of the Darjeeling hills in West Bengal can legally be sold as "Darjeeling." For importers, that protection is the product's biggest selling point — and the main thing to check before you buy.

This guide covers what importers, distributors, and private-label brands should know before placing a bulk order.

## 1. Know the flushes

Darjeeling tea is harvested in seasons called *flushes*, and each tastes different:

| Flush | Approx. season | Character |
|---|---|---|
| First flush | March–April | Light, floral, bright — highest demand, premium price |
| Second flush | May–June | Fuller body, the famous "muscatel" note |
| Monsoon | July–September | Stronger, darker, good value for blends |
| Autumnal | October–November | Mellow, rounded, coppery cup |

Many buyers combine a premium first or second flush line with a monsoon or autumnal tea for blends and value ranges.

## 2. Understand the grades

Grades describe leaf size and quality, not origin. Common ones you'll see on offer sheets:

- **SFTGFOP1 / FTGFOP1** — whole leaf with plenty of tips; top quality.
- **TGFOP / GFOP / FOP** — whole leaf, fewer tips.
- **BOP (Broken Orange Pekoe)** — broken leaf; stronger, faster brew.
- **Fannings / Dust** — small particles, mainly for tea bags.

Ask your supplier for the grade *and* the garden/flush on every lot.

## 3. Check authenticity

Because the name is protected, check that your supplier can show:
- The **Tea Board of India** Darjeeling certification mark / logo usage rights.
- Garden and invoice details for the lot.
- Food-safety and export registrations — Quantum Accord holds **FSSAI, FIEO, ISO, DGFT, GFE and GCCI** registrations.
