# First Flush vs Second Flush Darjeeling: A Buyer's Guide to Every Flush

"First flush or second flush?" is the most common question we hear from new Darjeeling buyers. The honest answer is: it depends on your customers. The two flushes come from the same gardens but taste like different teas, and they suit different products and price points.

This guide compares first and second flush, explains the monsoon and autumnal flushes too, and gives practical tips for importers planning a range.

## What is a "flush"?

A flush is a period of new growth on the tea bush. In Darjeeling, the bushes rest over the cold winter and then produce new shoots in waves through the year. Each wave is picked and processed separately, and each has its own character because of the weather, altitude and how the leaf has grown.

Approximate seasons (timing shifts each year with the weather):

| Flush | Approx. season | Cup character | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| First flush | March–April | Light, floral, bright, brisk | Premium loose leaf, specialty retail |
| Second flush | May–June | Fuller body, amber cup, "muscatel" | Premium loose leaf, gift ranges |
| Monsoon (rain) flush | July–September | Stronger, darker, less nuanced | Blends, tea bags, value lines |
| Autumnal flush | October–November | Mellow, rounded, coppery | Everyday loose leaf, blends |

## First flush: light, floral and seasonal

First flush is picked from the first shoots after winter. The cup is pale gold to light amber, with floral and green notes and a lively, brisk finish. Many first flush teas are processed with lighter oxidation than a typical black tea, so the dry leaf often looks greenish.

**Why buyers want it:** It's the most sought-after Darjeeling. Specialty retailers and tea lovers often wait for the new season's first flush, which typically gives it the highest prices of the year.

**What to watch:** Freshness matters. First flush is valued for its bright, delicate character, so storage and timing count. Plan your order early in the season and store it well.

## Second flush: the "muscatel" cup

Second flush follows a short break after first flush. The leaf has had more time to develop, and the cup is deeper amber with a fuller body. The best second flush teas have the well-known **muscatel** character — a ripe, grape-like note. This is often attributed in part to small insects feeding on the leaf, which the plant responds to, though weather and processing also play a role.

**Why buyers want it:** For many people, second flush is the "classic" Darjeeling taste. It's rich enough to take a little milk, yet still complex, which gives it broad appeal.

**What to watch:** Not every second flush lot is strongly muscatel. Taste before you buy if that's the note your customers expect.

## Monsoon and autumnal flushes: value and versatility

### Monsoon flush

The rainy season brings fast growth. Monsoon teas are generally stronger and darker, with less of the delicate aroma of the spring flushes. They're often used in blends, tea bags and value ranges, and typically cost less than first or second flush.

### Autumnal flush

After the rains, growth slows and the leaf develops a mellow, rounded, coppery cup, sometimes with nutty or woody notes. Autumnal teas are a good-value option for everyday Darjeeling, and some buyers prefer their smooth, gentle style.

## First flush vs second flush at a glance

- **Taste:** first flush is lighter and floral; second flush is fuller and fruity/muscatel.
- **Colour:** first flush brews pale gold; second flush brews deeper amber.
- **With milk:** first flush is best plain; second flush can take a splash of milk.
- **Price:** both are premium; the best first flush lots usually command the top prices.
- **Customer:** first flush suits specialty and connoisseur buyers; second flush suits a wider premium audience.

## Buying tips for importers

1. **Decide on your range first.** A common approach is one premium spring tea (first or second flush) plus a monsoon or autumnal tea for blends or a value line.

## FAQ

**Which is better, first flush or second flush?**
Neither is "better". First flush is lighter and floral; second flush is fuller and muscatel. Choose based on your customers' taste.

**Which flush has the most caffeine?**
Caffeine varies with leaf, grade and brewing, and there's no simple rule by flush. Avoid making caffeine claims on packs without lab testing.

**Is monsoon flush low quality?**
No. It's a different style — stronger and less aromatic — and it's well suited to blends and tea bags.
