# Darjeeling Tea Grades Explained: From SFTGFOP1 to Dust

Open any Darjeeling offer sheet and you'll meet a wall of letters: SFTGFOP1, FTGFOP1, TGFOP, BOP. To a new buyer it looks like a code. It is one, but it's a simple code once you know the pieces.

This guide explains what each letter means, how the main grades compare, and how to read an offer sheet so you can compare lots with confidence before you place a bulk or private-label order.

## Grade describes the leaf, not the origin

A grade tells you about the **size and appearance of the leaf** — whether it's whole or broken, and how much bud ("tip") it contains. It doesn't tell you where the tea was grown, which flush it came from, or exactly how it will taste.

That matters because the grade letters are used across many tea regions. "FTGFOP1" on its own doesn't mean Darjeeling. Origin is a separate question, and for Darjeeling it's protected by a Geographical Indication (see our [Darjeeling tea import guide](/blog/how-to-import-darjeeling-tea-from-india) for authenticity checks).

Grading is also not fully standardised. Producers apply the terms, so two gardens' FTGFOP1 lots can differ noticeably. Treat the grade as a starting point, not a guarantee.

## What the letters mean

Almost every whole-leaf grade is built on **OP — Orange Pekoe**. Despite the name, it has nothing to do with orange flavour. The origin of "orange" is commonly attributed to the Dutch House of Orange-Nassau, though historians debate it. In practice, OP simply refers to a whole-leaf grade made from the bud and young leaves.

The letters added in front build on that base:

| Letter | Stands for | What it signals |
|---|---|---|
| **OP** | Orange Pekoe | Whole leaf, the base grade |
| **F** | Flowery | Contains buds/tips |
| **G** | Golden | Tips show a golden or silvery colour after processing |
| **T** | Tippy | A generous proportion of tips |
| **F** (leading) | Finest | A higher selection within the grade |
| **S** | Special (sometimes "Super") | The highest selection |
| **1** | — | Top quality within that grade |
| **B** | Broken | Leaf has been broken into smaller pieces |

Read from right to left and the name expands naturally. For example, **FTGFOP1** is *Finest Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe, grade one*.

## The main Darjeeling grades, from top to bottom

### Whole-leaf grades

- **SFTGFOP1 — Special Finest Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe 1.** The top whole-leaf designation, with a high proportion of well-formed tips. Usually reserved for a garden's best lots.
- **FTGFOP1 — Finest Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe 1.** A very common premium grade for Darjeeling. Whole leaf with plenty of tips.
- **TGFOP — Tippy Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe.** Whole leaf with visible tips, but fewer than the grades above.
- **GFOP — Golden Flowery Orange Pekoe.** Whole leaf with some golden tips.
- **FOP — Flowery Orange Pekoe.** Whole leaf with buds, but a looser, more open leaf and fewer tips.

Whole-leaf grades usually suit loose-leaf retail, premium tins and specialty shops, where buyers want to see and brew the leaf.

### Broken grades

- **BOP — Broken Orange Pekoe.** Smaller, broken pieces of leaf. It brews faster and gives a stronger, more robust cup. You may also see variations like **FBOP** or **GFBOP**, which add the same letters as above to a broken grade.

Broken grades are often a good fit for everyday blends, larger tea bags and food-service packs.

### Fannings and dust

- **Fannings** — very small pieces left after the larger grades are sorted. They infuse quickly and are mainly used in tea bags.
- **Dust** — the finest particles. Very fast, strong infusion; used mostly in tea bags and some blends.

These grades are typically the most economical. They're not "bad" tea — they're made for a different purpose.

## Choosing the right grade for your product

- **Premium loose-leaf brand:** SFTGFOP1 or FTGFOP1, usually first or second flush.
- **Mid-range loose leaf or gift packs:** TGFOP or GFOP.
- **Blends and everyday packs:** BOP, often from monsoon or autumnal flush for value.
- **Tea bags:** fannings or dust, sometimes BOP for a "premium" bag.

## FAQ

**Is SFTGFOP1 always better than FTGFOP1?**
Not always. The grade describes leaf appearance. A well-made FTGFOP1 from a strong lot can taste better than a weaker SFTGFOP1. Always taste samples side by side.

**Does "Orange Pekoe" mean orange-flavoured tea?**
No. It's a leaf grade term with no link to citrus flavour.

**Are fannings and dust low quality?**
They're small-particle grades made for fast infusion, mainly in tea bags. Quality still varies by garden and flush.

**Does the grade prove the tea is from Darjeeling?**
No. Grade letters are used in many regions. Check origin separately through the GI certification mark and garden documents.
